Why some mixes turn muddy

No tube of paint is a pure primary. Every yellow, red and blue leans a little toward one neighbor. Cadmium red leans orange; alizarin leans violet. Lemon yellow leans green; cadmium yellow leans orange.

When both paints lean toward the color you want, you get a clean, saturated mix. When they lean away from it, each one sneaks in a bit of the third primary, and three primaries together make brown or gray.

That is the whole idea behind the split-primary palette: carry a warm and a cool version of each primary, and pick the pair that leans the right way.

Ratios are parts by volume. Start with the lighter paint and add the darker or stronger one in small amounts.

Painting statues: making stone breathe

Every art academy starts students at the same place: a plaster bust under one light. A statue holds perfectly still and has no color of its own to distract you, so it teaches the thing that matters most, which is light turning around a form. Get that right and cold stone looks warm enough to touch.

Bob Ross's 13 colors

For eleven years of The Joy of Painting, Bob Ross built nearly every painting from the same core set of thirteen oil paints. He painted whole landscapes, from sky to snow to shoreline, in about half an hour.

His palette proves the point of everything above: a small set of paints, known well, goes a very long way. Most of his colors are dark straight from the tube. Nearly every light value on screen came from working them into white.

He also treated a slip of the brush as an opening, not a failure. A stray mark could become a new tree or a rock. That's a good mindset for mixing, too.

Habits that make better color

Add dark to light

A speck of blue shifts a pile of white a long way. A pile of white barely moves a pile of blue. Start from the light paint and you waste less.

Treat staining pigments with respect

Phthalos and quinacridones are several times stronger than earth colors. Pick them up on the tip of the knife, not the brush.

Mute with a complement

To calm a color, add a touch of its opposite before reaching for black. Red plus a little green stays alive; red plus black often goes dead and cold.

Keep the palette small

Six to eight paints teaches you what each one does. Mixes from a limited palette also harmonize with each other, because they share ingredients.

Judge color dry and in place

Acrylics dry noticeably darker, and oils shift a little too. Test a stroke on scrap and hold it next to the colors it will sit beside.

Mix more than you need

Matching a mix exactly the second time is hard. Make extra, and wipe your knife between colors so the next pile stays clean.
